REVIEW OF THE FACEBOOK NETWORK ANALYSIS
6 November 2026

The arithmetic was checked again. The original centrality tables, clique
counts, community assignments and sensitivity results are unchanged. The
independent verification script and results/verification.json provide an
additional way to check the saved results.

Clarifications and improvements made:

1. All degree counts are explicitly ties inside the selected friend subgraph.
   A person's complete Facebook friend count is not observed here.
2. The six maximum cliques overlap and cover 15 distinct people. They must
   not be read as six disjoint groups. Clique counts use all 59 nodes.
3. The 14-group full-network Louvain output includes seven isolates. Their
   assignment has no effect on modularity. Community counts and seed-dependent
   partitions are not treated as externally verified social circles.
4. Graph labels are larger and selective, the main plot has a degree size key,
   and the degree bar chart uses integer ticks. All IDs and both F/M community
   memberships are retained in the full appendix and CSV files.
5. Whole-network and main-component statistics are separated in the summary
   table. Ambiguous slash-separated pairs were removed.
6. The betweenness sum explicitly excludes unreachable pairs. The eigenvector
   explanation gives the component eigenvalues supporting its scope limit.
   An equation now defines the adjusted Rand index used in the sensitivity check.
7. Report tables read numerical results instead of hardcoded basic counts.
   The analysis verifies the source archive hash and each edge's duplication
   pattern. Report building checks provenance and the stated Louvain settings.
8. Wording was tightened to describe measured structural roles without implying
   observed influence, communication, real-world group labels or statistical
   significance. Software docstrings and compilation errors were clarified.
9. A factual overstatement in the original limitations was corrected. The
   archive does provide ego-defined circle memberships (17 circles cover 58 of
   the 59 roster nodes). This report does not evaluate agreement with them;
   they are unused reference memberships, not unavailable data. Their anonymous
   names do not identify family, workplace or other social meaning.

Remaining limits are properties of the dataset and methods, not corrected by
editing: this is one historical, anonymised, binary friendship subgraph;
outside ties and the meanings of source circle labels are unknown; Louvain
is heuristic; and the community results vary across seeds. Agreement with
the provided circle memberships has not been assessed. The report states
these limits.
